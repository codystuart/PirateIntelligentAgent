###  What do computer scientists do and why does it matter?
- Computer scientists are problem-solvers who use the principles of computation, algorithms, and data structures to formally define, design, and analyze software, hardware, and complex systems. They are crucial because their work involves creating the efficient procedures, like the Deep Q-Learning algorithm used here, that underpin virtually all modern technology, driving innovation across fields from artificial intelligence to medicine and global communication.

### How do I approach a problem as a computer scientist?
- As a computer scientist, I would approach a problem using a structured and logical process rooted in abstraction and algorithmic design. First, I would abstract the problem to define it clearly, identifying the core components, like defining the maze as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with clear states and rewards. Then, I would design an algorithmic solution, which involves choosing or creating an efficient procedure, such as Deep Q-Learning, to systematically achieve the goal. Finally, I would implement and rigorously test the algorithm to ensure it is robust, refining the solution parameters until the desired performance is achieved.

### What are my ethical responsibilities to the end user and the organization?
- My ethical responsibilities require building systems that prioritize safety and trust for all stakeholders. To the end user, this means ensuring the AI is robust and safe, working reliably and predictably without causing harm, while maintaining transparency about the agent's decision-making process. For the organization, my responsibility is to prevent algorithmic bias by scrutinizing training data, and to uphold professional integrity by accurately communicating the agent's capabilities and limitations.
